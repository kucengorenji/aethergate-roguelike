# 0014: VFX in code

Status: done, with the WebGPU and step budget checks left for the WSL machine
Owner: Mikael (built by an agent in a cloud session, reviewed in the PR)

## Problem

Every effect in the game is still graybox: bolts are flat blue lozenges, impacts are translucent spheres, telegraphs are flat orange rings, statuses only tint the body, and nothing moves in the air.
The approved Round A frames (spec 0013) show the target: pale blue glintstone bolts with bright trails, glowing orange ground rings that warn of a strike, meteors falling on fiery trails, restrained bloom on magic, ash drifting through golden haze.
M6 says "a screenshot of any room looks like a game, not a prototype", and effects are most of what moves on screen.
VFX are built in code (no textures or models), so they do not wait for the asset waves.

## Behavior

Palette (from spec 0013): pale blue for the player's spells, orange-red for enemy eyes and telegraphs, green for thorn casters, violet starlight for the Starfallen Knight, warm gold light, ash in the air.
The four demigods tint what they bless: the Veiled Moon Queen frost blue, the Starfall General violet, the Rot Saint green, the Storm Warden yellow.

1. **Spell trails and impacts.**
   Every player bolt is a bright pale blue core with a glowing trail that starts at the staff, never behind it, and sheds small sparkles.
   The Glint Staff trail is medium, the Lance Staff trail is long, thin and bright, and Scatter bolts have short trails.
   A bolt that hits anything bursts in a small flash and a spray of sparks, sized by staff (Lance biggest, Scatter smallest).
   With a primary blessing (Moonlit, Starfall, Blighted or Arc Bolts) the trail, sparkles and impact take that demigod's color around a pale core.
   Caster orbs are green thorn magic with a green trail and burst; the training totem's orbs stay orange.
   Blessing effects that happen without a bolt get their own look: Pale Step's frost nova (a blue ring and ice shards), Meteor Step's falling-star slam (a violet and orange burst with a ring), Spore Step's green spore cloud, and crushes and the Collapsing Comet's pull as a violet implosion.
2. **The comet.**
   Casting flashes at the staff with a spray of sparks.
   In flight the comet has a thick ember trail and sheds embers and smoke.
   The burst is a bright flash, sparks, smoke and embers, and a shockwave ring on the ground that reaches the damage radius.
   Everything takes the Skill blessing's color, or warm orange without one.
3. **Status auras.**
   Chill: frost motes drift around the enemy (more with more stacks) on top of the existing blue tint; frozen enemies sit in a faceted ice shell.
   Weight: violet star motes fall onto the enemy and a violet ring under it tightens with each stack toward the crush.
   Rot: green spores rise and drip from the enemy, more with more stacks.
   Arcs: lightning is drawn as a jagged, flickering bolt, and the struck enemy crackles with yellow sparks.
4. **Hits, spawns and deaths.**
   Every landed hit on an enemy throws hot sparks from the struck side; a hit on the player throws red-orange sparks; a shield block throws gold sparks back at the attacker.
   Rising enemies come up through a glowing ring in their own glow color, with dark ash and embers rising around them.
   Dying enemies break into ash and embers while they sink; bosses do it bigger, with a shockwave ring in their color.
5. **Telegraph decals.**
   Every area attack draws an orange-red decal on the ground: a bright rim that shows the exact area and a fill that grows from the center (or from the attacker) and reaches the rim at the strike moment.
   Covered attacks: Ward Knight and Ward Captain slams, the Starfallen Knight's gravity well (the fill counts down to the detonation), its meteors, the Captain's charge path and the sweep cone.
   Smaller, fainter decals now also warn of the Hollow Soldier's sword arc and the Rot Hound's leap, so every attack has a ground decal as the design asks.
   Meteors fall visibly onto their decal during the last half second, on a fiery trail.
   In phase 3 the burning edge outside the closing ring is a band of fire with flames rising along its inner edge.
   The old graybox rings, strips and cones are gone.
6. **Ash in the air.**
   Fine ash flakes and a few glowing embers drift and fall around the player in every room and in the Kindled Hall, fading out at the edges of view.
7. **Post-processing.**
   Bright magic (bolt cores, trails, sparks, telegraph rims, enemy eyes in a wind-up, the hall's glowing stations) blooms softly; the stone and the sky do not.
   A warm color grade (warm highlights, violet-brown shadows, a little contrast and saturation, a soft vignette) is applied to every frame, on both the WebGPU and the WebGL 2 backends.
   Per-room fog and lighting stay as they are; they belong to the M6 integration wave.

### Rules that keep the game testable

- Paused screenshots stay deterministic: effects evolve with fixed steps and are drawn from simulation state.
- Effect randomness comes from its own seeded stream, so gameplay randomness and every seeded run keep their outcomes.
- Effects never change gameplay: no hit, timing or damage depends on them.

## Out of scope

- Anything under `assets-src/` or `public/assets/`: models, textures, audio, the style guide.
- The HUD and menus (`src/game/ui/`, spec 0015), including damage numbers.
- Per-room lighting, fog and post settings per room type (M6 Wave 2 integration).
- Dynamic lights for effects: adding or removing lights rebuilds every material, which costs too much on the target iGPU.

## Open questions

- Low keeps the color grade (one cheap full-screen pass) so every quality shares the same look, and only drops bloom; say so if Low should be the raw render instead.
- The new sword-arc and leap decals make soldiers and hounds a little easier to read than in the M3 playtest; they are faint and can be turned off with one tunable if the fight gets too easy.
- Fog color stays as it is; a warmer fog tint per room type fits better in the Wave 2 lighting pass than in a global grade.
